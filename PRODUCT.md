# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary user: a non-technical founder, CEO, or first PM at a 2-15 person startup. They own customer conversations, roadmap decisions, investor updates, and support escalations, but they do not live inside GitHub.

Secondary user: the developer or small engineering team shipping the product. They want their work to be visible without writing status updates or being interrupted for translation.

Current demo context: an ABM startup shipping account intelligence, Salesforce/HubSpot integrations, enrichment jobs, and sales workflow tooling.

## Product Purpose

Bridgr is a release intelligence layer. The PM sets the product focus in plain language, then Bridgr reads versioned engineering releases from connected repositories and translates them into plain-English product impact: what changed, who is affected, and what the PM or customer team needs to do.

The product exists because engineering tools are written for the sender, while the person making product and customer decisions needs a receiver-first view. Success means a PM can open the platform, see whether releases support the current product focus, choose the customer-facing next step, and mark that action as planned.

## Brand Personality

Precise, restrained, authoritative.

The interface should feel like a calm product command center: dark, editorial, premium, and useful. It treats founders as peers. It does not over-explain, moralize, or decorate for its own sake.

## Anti-references

- Not Jira, Linear, or GitHub issue tracking.
- Not engineering analytics, DORA metrics, productivity scoring, or developer monitoring.
- Not Notion-style softness, warm whites, beige surfaces, or friendly document aesthetics.
- Not colorful gradients, decorative dashboards, empty motion, or generic SaaS hero language.
- Not digest-first or sprint-planning-first for the demo.

## Design Principles

1. Signal first, evidence second: the first screen answers whether action is needed before showing the feed.
2. PM input stays light: the PM sets one product focus, not a full sprint plan.
3. PM action is explicit: the PM can turn a release into a planned customer action.
4. Built for the reader: every release is translated into account impact, customer scope, action, and risk.
5. Same truth, different lens: PM and developer see the same release reality, with permissions and wording adjusted by role.
6. No jargon without translation: technical release notes may appear, but never as the primary meaning.
7. Premium utility through restraint: depth comes from layout, border, hierarchy, and surgical color, not ornament.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast. Motion must support reduced-motion preferences. Color may indicate status, but status must also be written in text. UI copy should stay concise and plain enough for non-technical operators under time pressure.
